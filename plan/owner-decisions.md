# Owner decisions

Every product decision the owner made for the Django migration, newest last. The issue named after each decision holds the full text, and #4 holds D1 to D13. ADRs in `server/docs/adr/` record the architectural ones. Add a decision here in the same change that edits the issues it touches.

| D1 | 2026-10-21 | SQLite in WAL mode is the default. Postgres is first class, through a compose profile and `DATABASE_URL`. CI runs both. | #4, ADR-05 |
| D2 | 2026-10-21 | One database. `Event` rows, a site-level active-event pointer, and an `event` foreign key on every event-scoped model (option b of #9). | #4, #9 |
| D3 | 2026-10-21 | Docker is the only deployment, on Python 3.14. Laptops connect timers through the browser timer (Web Serial). | #4, ADR-03, ADR-14 |
| D4 | 2026-10-21 | GPRM, MS Access, and ODBC are removed. CSV roster import and the importer for old DerbyNet SQLite files stay. Pi timer and photo-stand images (#59) go to the backlog. | #4, ADR-06 |
| D5 | 2026-10-21 | Only kiosk pages, standings, and the data they fetch are anonymous. Everything else needs a login, including replay and the browser timer. | #4, #15 |

| D26 | 2026-10-24 | The timer audit never delays the timer reply. The audit row is written after the race-state transaction, with at most a 1 s lock wait on SQLite. On timeout or error it is skipped with a warning. | #20, #27, #32 |
| D27 | 2026-10-24 | A result changed during an intermission rebuilds the waiting final's roster and chart if none of its heats has run. Otherwise the dashboard warns. | #20, #25 |
| D28 | 2026-10-24 | A final with fewer than 2 racers keeps the intermission open with "final needs at least 2 racers", and the coordinator skips the final or changes the cut. | #20, #25 |
| D29 | 2026-10-25 | No Raspberry Pi timing is required. Speed targets are measured on a laptop, because a Pi is not supported as the server (D21). | #22, #26 |
| D30 | 2026-10-25 | Master heats are renumbered inside every chart change and only then. A failed `schedule.generate` renumbers nothing (Q18), and a playlist round is scheduled through `generate_schedule`, so it gets master heat numbers (Q19). | #20, #100 |
| D31 | 2026-10-25 | All three group formation rules stay. By partition is the default and the owner's rule, and custom starts from the current groups. A real rule change deletes every round, schedule, group award, and playlist step, after the group editor warns what it deletes. Applying the rule already in force does nothing, and a rename changes nothing else. A rule change is refused once any heat has a result. A partition name that matches another with case ignored is refused with `name-taken` on every database. | #25, #43 |
| D32 | 2026-10-25 | A late racer for a running round joins only when the race coordinator presses Add to schedule after a preview, and is fitted into the remaining heats. For a racer who missed round 1, check-in can only request a place in the final, and the race coordinator allows or declines. An allowed racer is extra, is fitted into the final like a late racer, and stays through D27 rebuilds. Once a heat of the final has run, round 1 results are locked for everyone except the race coordinator. If the race coordinator changes one, the dashboard suggests finalists to add or remove and changes nothing itself, and a racer who qualifies this way stays eligible for speed awards. | #24, #26, #27, #34, #41, #42, #103 |
| D33 | 2026-10-25 | A racer has two award settings. "Not eligible for any award" is the legacy `exclude`, and "not eligible for speed awards" is new. Speed awards are the awards placed by time. A racer who missed round 1 starts not eligible for speed awards, and the race coordinator can set either setting for any racer added late. A racer who is not eligible shows on the standings marked not eligible. Eligible to race means the racer passed check-in, where the race crew weighed the car and inspected its dimensions. | #26, #33, #41, #42, #45, #49, #52, #103 |
| D34 | 2026-10-26 | An award's scope is one partition, or all partitions that count for all-partition awards. Each partition has that setting, on by default, so a Parents or Siblings partition can be left out of awards such as fastest in the pack. A final's cut skips any racer who cannot win that group's speed awards and takes the next eligible racer, and the skipped racer's times still show on the standings, marked not eligible. Fitting a racer into a round never changes the running heat or the one staged heat, and adds heats at the end when no later heat is left. At registration and check-in a racer's partition is picked from the existing partitions. The legacy importer renames a partition whose name matches an earlier one with case ignored and reports it, and a racer import naming a case variant joins the existing partition. D28's message covers a final with zero racers too, except a waiting round deleted during the intermission, which says its roster is gone. | #24, #26, #29, #33, #42, #43, #45, #49, #52, #103, #104 |
| D35 | 2026-10-26 | Every award lists the partitions it can go to, all partitions by default. A speed award's places come from the times of racers in its list only, and a judged award can go only to a racer in its list. This replaces D34's per-partition setting "counts for all-partition awards". A list left at all partitions picks up partitions added later, and a narrowed list does not. Deleting a partition removes it from every list, and a list left empty goes back to all partitions. A final's cut uses the list of the group the final is for, never the pack's. A racer who is not eligible shows on the standings with their times, marked not eligible, and gets no place number, so the other racers are numbered as if that racer were absent. | #26, #33, #43, #45, #49, #52, #106 |
| D36 | 2026-10-26 | A car keeps racing all its heats when its racer has gone home, and it can still qualify for a final. A car leaves heats only when it is taken off the roster or its check-in is reverted. When a racer passes check-in while a round of their group is running, check-in asks whether to add the car to the current round. The race crew or the race coordinator can answer. Yes fits the car into the remaining heats, and the racer is eligible as usual. No leaves the car out of that round, and the dashboard lists it in case the race coordinator adds it before the round ends. This replaces D32's dashboard notice as the main way a late racer joins. A second request to add a car that already has heats is refused. A reschedule is refused when the schedule uses more lanes than the track has. A search for a late racer always tries its last added heat, a late racer may race alone in the heats added at the end, and a preview never reloads the kiosks. The staged heat is only ever in the current round. The race coordinator can close a damaged lane during a round. The times run on that lane in that round are dropped, and the remaining heats are rescheduled so that every racer gets the round's runs per lane on each open lane only, without changing the running or staged heat. Closing a lane asks how to score the round, for example keeping all times instead of dropping the slowest. That choice and the closed lane carry into the following rounds until the race coordinator changes them, and a closed lane can be reopened only before the next round is scheduled. | #24, #27, #29, #41, #42, #107, #108 |
| D37 | 2026-10-26 | Reopening a closed lane leaves the rounds that closed it as they finished, with their dropped times still dropped, and only later rounds use the lane. In an abbreviated round, a car whose only time is dropped by a closed lane gets one new run on an open lane. Under points racing, the places in a heat with a dropped lane move up, so the heat scores as if it ran on the open lanes. A car taken off the roster, for example because it is too damaged to run, leaves the running and staged heats too, and its lane runs empty; the other cars in those heats keep their lanes. | #24, #107, #108, #105 |
| D38 | 2026-10-27 | An award's list offers only the partitions its racers can come from. For the pack's awards that is every partition, and for a group's award it is the partitions in that group. "All partitions" is its own choice on an award, and it always means every partition the award can draw from, so it picks up a partition added to the event or moved into the group later. When deleting a partition, or moving it out of a group, leaves a narrowed list holding every partition the award can draw from, the list goes back to "all partitions". The glossary terms stay as they are. A partition is what a racer registers into, such as one den or Parents, and a subgroup is one partition or several partitions ranked together. This extends D35. | #26, #43, #106 |
| D39 | 2026-10-27 | A reschedule never changes the running heat or the staged heat, even when every car in one of them has been taken off the roster. The emptied heat stays in the schedule with its number, and the race crew runs or skips it by hand. Under the master schedule, the protected staged heat is the heat that runs next on the track, even when it is in another round than the one being rescheduled. Adding a late racer to a round is refused when the racer is not on that round's roster, and the race coordinator puts the racer on the roster first. | #20, #24, #105 |
| D40 | 2026-10-27 | An emptied heat held under D39 is let go once the race crew moves the current heat to another round and the emptied heat's own round has no heat after it. Its number is then free, and moving to the next heat never lands on it again. | #27 |
| D41 | 2026-10-27 | D32's lock on round results also applies to a combined round. Once a heat of a round drawn from several groups' rounds, such as Den 2 and Den 3 together, has a result, the results of the rounds it was drawn from lock as they would for a final. The race coordinator can still change them. | #27, #109 |
| D42 | 2026-10-27 | A car the timer reports no time for did not finish. When the timer reports a heat with a lane that has a car but no time, or a time that is not a number, that lane gets a time of 9.999 seconds, the heat counts as run, and racing moves on as usual, so a car that does not finish in a round's last heat never stops the playlist. When the track or another car caused the car not to finish, the race coordinator reruns the heat, and every car races again in the same lanes. | #27, #109 |
| D43 | 2026-10-27 | Setting up a final always asks how many racers go to it, whether it starts from the playlist or the race coordinator builds it by hand, and the top racers of the round before it are picked. The final keeps that number, so finalist suggestions after a changed result work the same for every final. | #103, #110 |
| D44 | 2026-10-27 | A racer allowed into a final goes into the next round their group races in, whether that is the group's own final or a combined round drawn from several groups' rounds. | #103, #110 |
| D45 | 2026-10-28 | Closing a lane applies to every round that has started and still has a heat left to run, so under the master schedule each interleaved round drops that lane's times and reschedules its remaining heats on the open lanes. A round that is scheduled but has not started is rescheduled on the open lanes as part of the closure. The scoring chosen when the lane closes applies to the rounds being closed and to every round with no result yet, and finished rounds keep the scoring they had. | #108, #116 |
| D46 | 2026-10-28 | Under points racing, every round scores out of the lanes it actually races on, whether a lane was switched off in the settings or closed during racing, so first place on a 4-lane track with one lane unused gets 3 points. PHP scores out of the track's lane count. | #108, #116 |
| D47 | 2026-10-28 | A car number is a whole number, and Django refuses any other value with `invalid-carnumber`. The new pages show a car number padded to three digits, such as 001 and 042, while storage and the legacy API keep plain integers. | #29, #42, #123 |
| D48 | 2026-10-28 | Each partition holds an ordered list of car number ranges that the race coordinator sets, which needs `SET_UP`. A partition with no range uses the `car-numbering` setting. Check-in suggests the next free number from the first range with room. A typed number outside the ranges, or a duplicate, is accepted with a warning. When every range is full the server answers `range-full` with the partition id, check-in asks for another range such as 400 to 499, and a new range may not overlap a range of another partition. | #29, #42, #43, #123 |
| D49 | 2026-10-28 | Under the by-partition rule, renaming a subgroup also renames its group and its partition, as renaming a group or a partition already does. PHP leaves the group name alone (C49). | D57 | 2026-10-29 | A new event's folders are `events/<slug>`, with `racers`, `cars`, `videos`, `logs`, `imagery` and `slides` inside, not PHP's `<year>/<name>`.
