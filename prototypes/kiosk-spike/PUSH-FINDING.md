## Push finding from the kiosk front-end spike (#71)

> *This was generated by AI during the spike.*

The #71 spike measured whether polling can keep two kiosk TVs animating in step, and what each candidate front end needs to start an animation from a push notice. Full results are on #71. The code is in `prototypes/kiosk-spike/` on branch `spike-71-kiosk-frameworks`.

**Polling cannot keep TVs in step.** Two TVs show a step one poll cycle apart, depending on how their cycles happen to line up.
- **Now racing** (`poll` 500 ms after each response, about a 515 ms cycle): two TVs whose cycles stayed independent were 239–276 ms apart. With random phases, expect a mean of about 170 ms and a maximum of about 515 ms.
- **Steps sent through `poll.kiosk`** (every 5 s), such as the standings reveal: two pairs measured 2.40 s and 0.14 s apart on every step. Expect a mean of about 1.7 s and a maximum of 5 s, fixed for as long as both TVs stay up. A TV starts a step 2.0–3.4 s after the operator, on average.
- **A caveat.** On `runserver`, two now-racing poll chains sometimes drifted into lock-step, 10–30 ms apart. The likely cause, unverified, is that overlapping requests finish together in one Python process. That cannot be counted on under gunicorn with several workers.

**Push as #56 proposes it closes the gap.** The server sends a notice that carries no state, and each TV then runs the fetch it already makes. The spike ran this as a server-sent event stream (`/spike/events`, which checks a change signature every 50 ms) under `runserver`.

| Page | TV-to-TV skew, push | TV-to-TV skew, polling | Latency to animation start, push |
|---|---|---|---|
| Now racing | mean 8–9 ms, max 19 ms | expected mean about 170 ms, max about 515 ms | 43–61 ms mean |
| Standings step | mean 8 ms, max 51 ms | expected mean about 1.7 s, max 5 s | 36–41 ms mean |

Both candidates measured the same. The latency includes up to 50 ms of the spike's database check, so a notice sent by the write itself would be faster.

**Request load.** One now-racing TV sent 129–135 polls in about 65 s with polling, and 20–21 with push and a 5 s fallback chain.

**What a page needs to animate from a notice:**
- **htmx + Alpine.js** (the spike's recommendation): the htmx SSE extension (2.9 KB, `htmx-ext-sse`). Put `sse-connect` on the page and add `sse:change` to the element's existing `hx-trigger`. That is no JavaScript. The notice issues the same fragment request a poll would, and the animation follows from the swap.
- **Svelte:** `new EventSource(url).addEventListener('change', poll)`, calling the function the poll chain already calls.
- **Either:** keep the poll chain as the fallback at a slower rate (the spike used 5 s). The replay pause must also hold back notice-driven fetches; the spike's pages skip a fetch while paused, whatever triggered it.

**Inputs for #56's design:**
1. **Topics.** A notice per topic (`race`, `kiosk:<address>`) with a version number is enough. The kiosk does not need the payload in the notice.
2. **Connections.** Each TV holds one open connection. `runserver` spent one thread per connection. #58's gunicorn sync workers cannot hold these connections, so the transport choice (ASGI, threads or gevent, plus `proxy_buffering off` in nginx) is the cost to measure.
3. **Tighter sync is possible but not needed.** 8 ms is below what a viewer notices across a gym. If it is ever needed, the notice could carry a "start at server time T" field that each TV waits for. The spike did not need it.
